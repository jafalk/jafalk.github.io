The obvious solution

One platform works as long as everything lives in one place

Putting all agents with one vendor is the fastest choice and often a sensible first step. But it is worth knowing the limit before the architecture is locked.

A

The platform governs

Orchestration, rules and logs sit with the vendor.

  • Works best when the data already lives in the platform
  • A split landscape creates blind spots
  • The rules follow the vendor, not you
  • Switching means rebuilding the rules
B

You own the couplings

The dependency map, the rules and the trail sit with you. The platforms execute.

  • Platforms can be chosen and replaced as needed
  • The same rules apply across platforms
  • Handovers between agents have an owner
  • The trail holds, even when the vendor changes

The plan

Four steps. The dependency map before the choice.

The first two steps are delivered with Looplo Custody and need no new software. They are what make the next choices cheaper and easier to justify.

01

Build the dependency map

Today: There is no single list of which agents are running, which systems they are connected to, and who granted the access.

An overview of agents, MCP connections and access across platforms - with a named owner on each line.

02

Describe the handovers

Today: Agents use each other's answers without anyone having decided what an answer may be used for.

A register of where agents call each other and the systems, what the handover may lead to, and when a person must step in.

03

Shared rules across platforms

Today: Each platform has its own rules for agents. They stop applying when the agent crosses into another.

Rules for what an agent may do, on whose behalf and with which data - written once and valid on any platform.

04

Choose the platform from the dependency map

Today: The orchestration tool is chosen first, and the architecture is adapted afterwards.

An independent basis for choosing platform and vendor, where the requirements come from your dependency map, not from the sales material.

The dependency map does not go out of date when the platform changes. Licences and tools come and go. The overview of what your agents may do, and who owns it, is yours.
